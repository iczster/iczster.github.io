## 🕹️ Atari Retro Collection Project

My **first ever console** was an **Atari 2600**, the classic **woodgrain version**, so when Atari announced the **Atari 2600+**, I jumped at the chance to relive some serious childhood memories.

Yes, original consoles are still out there, but the appeal of the **2600+** was obvious straight away:

- **HDMI output** for modern TVs  
- **Smaller, cleaner form factor**  
- **Compatibility with Atari 2600 and 7800 cartridges**  

Within minutes of unboxing, I was straight back in the late 70s and early 80s, joystick in hand, rediscovering games I hadn’t touched in decades.

## 📺 Why the Atari 2600+ Works So Well

The beauty of the 2600+ is how effortlessly it bridges old and new. No RF tuning, no signal issues, just plug in via HDMI and you’re playing instantly. It keeps the physical cartridge experience intact while removing all the modern-day friction.

Another big win is **7800 support**. I’ve already picked up a couple of 7800 cartridges, **Pac-Man** and **Galaga**, and plan to expand that side of the collection over time.

## 🛠️ Custom Shelf & Display

As part of this rebuild, I also **designed a custom shelf**, built specifically to store and display the cartridges I wanted to collect. The idea wasn’t to own everything, but to curate a **personal “greatest hits” collection** of games I actually played and loved growing up.

I spent a bit of time measuring and planning what this would look like and sourced the Atari badge. Having put the measurements together and worked out how I wanted it to look and where to house it then I passed it over to a joiner to do the build.


The end result looks fantastic, and best of all, **within seconds I can be playing Atari games again**. No emulation menus, no boot times, just pure retro gaming.

Some cartridges aren’t cheap these days. **H.E.R.O.** in particular was a bit painful on the wallet, but completely worth it. Seeing the carts lined up on the shelf feels just as satisfying as playing them.

## 🔮 What’s Next?

This project has completely reignited my love for **retro hardware**, not just emulation. There’s something special about physical cartridges, original controllers and the limitations of early game design.

More **retro projects are definitely coming soon**, including expanding the Atari 7800 side of the collection and revisiting other classic systems.

For now though, I’m more than happy knowing that a slice of gaming history is always just a power button away.
